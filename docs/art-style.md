# Art style

How images for Night City Tales are made, so every picture in the game looks
like it came from the same art department. Read this before generating any new
image, and add to it when a new kind of image joins the game.

There are three looks. Pick the one that matches where the image is shown.

| Look         | Used for                                                                                  | Where it is defined                                                                |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vignette** | Small gameplay illustrations of a moment: scenes, places on the map, flavor art           | The wrapper below                                                                  |
| **Backdrop** | Large images behind interface text: character-creation venues, scenes and chapter banners | The wrapper below                                                                  |
| **Portrait** | Character portraits, generated in the app                                                 | `HOUSE_LOOK` in `src/features/chargen/portraitPrompt.ts` (never generated by hand) |

The three share one palette and one finish: a painterly digital oil painting
with a matte-painting finish, late-80s/90s neon-noir cyberpunk, grounded and
lived-in, no text or logos. The vignette and backdrop wrappers are written for
ChatGPT's image generator. Paste the whole wrapper with the subject filled in,
every time. Each image is its own conversation turn, so no earlier image steers
the composition.

## Format

ChatGPT makes landscape images at 3:2. The app crops them with
`object-cover`, so:

- **Vignettes** can use the whole frame.
- **Backdrops** are cropped to a wide strip, so everything that matters goes in
  the middle horizontal band. The backdrop wrapper says so.
- Name the file exactly as listed. Either upload it through Lovable like every
  other asset (it lands in `src/assets/<name>.asset.json`), or commit the file
  itself into `src/assets/creator/`. A slot with no file falls back to the
  plain look, so images can arrive in any order.

## Vignette wrapper

Replace `[fill in]` with the subject.

```text
Painterly cyberpunk vignette illustration for a game UI, rendered as a digital oil painting with visible brush texture and a matte-painting finish. Not photorealistic, not a 3D render, not anime cel shading. High-end cinematic concept art quality, with the spirit of late-1980s and 1990s cyberpunk and Blade Runner-style neon noir, but grounded, human, and lived-in. This image is meant to be shown fairly small in the app, so prioritize strong readability, a clear focal point, and simple visual storytelling. The scene should communicate one specific moment or situation immediately at a glance. Favor a compact composition with 1–3 primary subjects maximum, clear silhouettes, readable body language, and a visually obvious main action or emotional beat. Use cinematic lighting with soft neon bloom, screen and signage glow, wet surfaces, reflective metal, and atmospheric depth, but keep the image fairly bright and readable so details are easy to pick out even at small size. Avoid overly dark shadows, muddy contrast, or excessive visual clutter. The environment should support the moment and feel richly cyberpunk, but it should not overwhelm the subject. Prefer medium-shot or close environmental compositions over huge wide establishing shots. Avoid giant empty areas and avoid overcrowding the image with too many people or too many competing points of interest. The overall tone should feel dangerous, intimate, human, and grounded rather than exaggerated or overly flashy. This is a reusable gameplay illustration, not a poster. No text, no logos, no watermarks, no captions, no collage, no second-person perspective. DO NOT let previous images influence the composition of this one. Subject / situation: [fill in]
```

## Backdrop wrapper

Replace `{calm}` with the side that text sits on (for example `left third`)
and `{place}` with the place.

```text
Painterly cyberpunk environment backdrop for a game UI, rendered as a digital oil painting with visible brush texture and a matte-painting finish. Not photorealistic, not a 3D render, not anime cel shading. High-end cinematic concept art quality, with the spirit of late-1980s and 1990s cyberpunk and Blade Runner-style neon noir, but grounded, human, and lived-in. This image is shown large, behind interface text, and will be cropped to a wide strip, so keep every important detail inside the middle horizontal band of the frame and let the top and bottom edges stay quiet. There are no people or figures anywhere in the scene: the place itself is the subject, and it should feel like somebody just left or is about to arrive. Keep the {calm} of the frame calmer, a little darker and low in detail so text laid over it stays readable, and put the visual interest on the other side. Use cinematic lighting with soft neon bloom, screen and signage glow, wet surfaces, reflective metal and atmospheric haze, with a clear mood but without crushed blacks or muddy contrast. Favor an intimate, eye-level view of one specific place over a huge establishing shot unless the place asked for is the skyline itself. The overall tone should feel dangerous, intimate, human, and grounded rather than exaggerated or overly flashy. No text, no readable signage, no logos, no watermarks, no captions, no collage. DO NOT let previous images influence the composition of this one. Landscape format. Place: {place}
```

### Must-have: the six fixers at the meet

The first screen deals three of these six, side by side, so what matters is
that no two look alike. The in-app portrait look (`HOUSE_LOOK`) asks for
"waist-up, facing the camera" against a hazy city, and every cast portrait
came out as the same picture with a different face. These break that on
purpose: each fixer is in their own venue, in a different pose, from a
different angle, under a different light.

These are only for the meet. Everywhere else — avatars, dossiers, the combat
HUD — keeps the square-on portrait in `public/images/cast/`, because an
off-centre pose crops badly into a small circle.

- **Size:** 1024 × 1536 (portrait, 2:3). The card shows it at 4:5, so about
  the top and bottom tenth is cropped away, and the name and a line of text
  sit over the bottom third.
- **Keep the face between roughly 12% and 45% of the way down the frame.**
  Nothing that matters in the bottom third.
- **Attach that fixer's current portrait** from `public/images/cast/<id>.webp`
  to the ChatGPT message, so it is recognisably the same person.
- Save as `meet-<id>.png` in `src/assets/creator/`.
