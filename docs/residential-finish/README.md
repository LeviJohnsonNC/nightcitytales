# Residential finish: art commission and implementation plan

Started from main `fbadf748464fcefa0da61e9be219ffa3f8ed86db`, after PR 306.
Branch: `codex/residential-finish`. Implementation and connected-Chrome review are complete; see
[`../checkpoint-residential-finish.md`](../checkpoint-residential-finish.md). Final visual acceptance remains with Levi.

The audit and original commission contract below record the starting point.

## Audit

The residential block takes up much of seed 7's left side. Its roof and wall are
large continuous fields; its windows have procedural occupancy, but their 12 cm
recesses, 7 cm sill projections and stroke-only floor divisions read weakly.
The annex already has commissioned window and shutter art. Reuse it.

There is a consistency gap to fix alongside the finish: `paintBuilding` calls
`paintHomeWall` and `paintHomeWindow`, but `paintCutawayWall` currently carries
the residential base material only. Its detailed clipped facade path is for
commercial art. The residential finish must share one painter between whole
and revealed walls, preserving the same source coordinates and clipping to
the kept segment; improving only the whole building would repeat this problem.

The glass dimensions come from `buildingFaces.ts`: the existing upper opening
is 1.4 × 1.2 m with a 5 cm frame on each side. The images supply the view behind
the frame. Code keeps the frame, central mullion, sill, head, recess and light.
No seams or structural bars are delegated to image generation. Wide ground-floor
bays keep their correctly sized procedural privacy treatment initially; do not
stretch these upper-window images across them.

Attach the window layout to each window prompt. Attach the masonry scale guide
only to the masonry prompt. The project's reference may also be attached for
brushwork/material style, but never for camera or lighting. Upload the four
original PNGs to this conversation, not screenshots or resized previews. The
source destination and importer will be set during integration; originals must
be excluded from the character-creator asset glob.

## Implementation order once art is returned

1. Review art against guides; crop and prefilter using the existing art pipeline.
   Verify masonry seams visually with a 3 × 3 repeat. Reject baked illumination.
2. Share the residential facade drawing between full and cutaway pieces. Add
   substantial but restrained sill/head planes and floor bands within each
   building's visual bounds. Retain all opening and entrance coordinates.
3. Use existing plaster above a masonry base below the ground-floor sill, with
   localized wear and contact shade. Carry the material relationship onto the
   attached annex without overwriting its shutter/window art or saved entrance.
4. Map the three window interiors to existing deterministic occupancy choices.
   Preserve the at-most-three lit-home budget and the shop as the primary light.
   Match the light mask to the painted interior rather than glowing over blinds.
5. Critique matched actual-browser views of seeds 7, 8 and 0 at normal play zoom,
   close-up, neutral, lights off, reveal and mixed damage. Check targeting,
   movement readability, asset fallback, and frozen save/load restoration.

No recipe revision, new upper floors, new entrances, collision or saved-object
changes are needed. Avoid decoration that only reads in a close-up.

## Integration

Levi uploaded all four originals in main commit `0e5d5bf8`. Their actual resolution
is 1254 square. `bun tools/art/residential-finish.ts` registers each painting to
its own measured glass bounds, then emits 468 × 396 interiors and a 512-square
masonry tile. Crops are recorded in the importer; the guide's 1024-pixel rectangle
is not blindly applied to these differently sized originals.

`paintHomeFace` now owns both full and retained residential faces, including
plaster, masonry, bands, openings and saved facade doors. Retained faces clip to
their actual span and height. The annex reuses the plaster/base and keeps its
existing commissioned window and shutter. The current loader, prefilter and
material grading paths are reused. Missing interior art keeps procedural cloth;
missing masonry keeps a flat plinth. Source PNGs are excluded from creator art.

Browser verification used the already-running local Vite server on port 8080 and
the connected Chrome browser. No local Chrome launch was needed. Evidence and
limitations are recorded in `../evidence/residential-finish/README.md`.
